Learn OKRs with Phoebe

How to measure people and teams fairly

Goals, metrics, and performance - done in a way that builds trust instead of gaming. This course teaches OKRs and fair performance measurement end to end, using an online retailer, Cartwheel, as the worked example. A leader track for C-level and upper management on designing the system, and a manager track for middle management and team leads on running it fairly. You will meet the biases that corrupt ratings, the law that guarantees any single metric gets gamed, where AI genuinely helps (and where it becomes surveillance), and a cadence that steers without smothering. You even drive a live Fair-Metric Lab. For everyone else, it is a plain-English guide to how good measurement actually works.

An honest note on measuring people: an OKR score is for steering and learning, never a direct input to pay - the moment it drives compensation, people game it and stop being honest. This course teaches from the canon (Grove, Doerr, Google re:Work, the Balanced Scorecard, DORA) and the fairness research (the idiosyncratic rater effect, organizational justice, bias in feedback language). AI is taught as a bias-checking assistant, never a judge: worker-management AI is high-risk under the EU AI Act and "solely automated" evaluation is a legal red line (GDPR Article 22). Cartwheel is a fictional teaching company and its benchmarks are illustrative. Regulations are summarized for awareness, not legal advice.

The rating measures the rater

16 sessions on measuring people and teams fairly, worked on one online retailer, Cartwheel.
SAME PERSONone ratee
SAME EVIDENCEone body of work
SAME CRITERIAone rubric
SAME QUARTERone period
SAME FORMone scale, 0 to 1
WHAT A RATING ACTUALLY MEASURES
Rater idiosyncrasy~55%
Actual performance~21%
Situation and error~24%
THE SOURCE
Scullen, Mount & Goff (2000) found 50 to 60 percent of rating variance is the rater. One score from one manager is a weak signal about a person.
SO THE FIX IS STRUCTURAL
01More than one perspective
02Criteria fixed in advance
03Evidence attached to claims
04Calibration across raters
THE ANATOMY, FROM SESSION A2
An Objective is qualitative: one memorable sentence about where you want to be. Its two to three Key Results are the measurable outcomes that prove you got there. If you cannot put a number on a Key Result, it is not a Key Result.
0-1Key Results score on one scale
~0.7The sweet spot, a Google norm to adapt, not a law
C/ACommitted goals differ from aspirational ones
WHAT IT IS NOT
Not that raters lie, and not that performance cannot be measured. Real differences exist. They are simply a smaller share of the variance than we assume.
Five instruments. One input. Five readings. The gap is mostly them, not the person.
