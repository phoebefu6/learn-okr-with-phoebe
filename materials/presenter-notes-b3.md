# Presenter notes - Manager session 3: Choosing fair metrics (Fair-Metric Lab)

Audience: middle management and team leads. This is the interactive session - the live Fair-Metric
Lab is here and nowhere else. Budget the demo time properly.

## Before the session
- Drive the lab yourself the night before. Click all six metrics, toggle the pay switch on each,
  and know the shape of every response before you are live.
- Expected behaviour, verified: gameable metrics (tickets closed, lines of code, hours logged)
  score 29 to 36 and open a 58 to 61 point gap when tied to pay, with the surrogation message.
  Fair metrics (CSAT plus first-contact resolution, DORA pair, add-to-cart on owned pages) score
  85 to 87 and stay within 13 to 19 points, with the "it held up" message.
- Know the one thing that confuses everyone: the pay toggle does NOT change the fairness score. It
  changes the chart. The score is a property of the metric's design; the gap is what happens when
  you attach reward. Say this before the first toggle, not after someone asks.
- Test the page on the actual projector. The chart is the session.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap - from checklist to instinct
- 3-14: Part 1 - when a measure becomes a target; Goodhart and surrogation
- 14-18: Part 2 - score every metric before you set it
- 18-38: The Fair-Metric Lab, driven live
- 38-42: Worked example - turn a gameable metric into a fair one
- 42-45: Q&A

## Demo run-of-show
- Open on tickets closed with the toggle OFF. Both lines rise together. Establish that the metric
  looks fine when nothing is riding on it - that is the trap.
- Then toggle pay ON. The measured line races to target, the true outcome falls away, the gap
  label appears. Pause on the divergence and let the room say what happened before you explain.
- Now switch to CSAT plus first-contact resolution with the toggle still on. It holds. That
  contrast, same toggle, opposite result, is the lesson: fair metrics survive becoming a target.
- Run hours logged last if the room includes anyone enthusiastic about activity tracking.
- Say the honesty rail out loud: the rubric scoring is real, the Goodhart model is a deterministic
  teaching simulation on a worked Cartwheel example, not a live HR system.

## Watch for
- The room concluding "so never tie anything to pay." Closer: never tie a gameable single number
  to pay, and pair everything you do attach.
- Someone asking to change the rubric weights. Good instinct, out of scope live - point at the
  six-point checklist as the transparent version.
- "Police it harder" as the answer to gaming. The lab answer is pairing, not policing. The quiz
  tests exactly this.
- Time overrun. The lab is worth 20 minutes; the worked example is the thing to sacrifice.

## Cuts if long
- Part 2 compresses to the scoring habit stated in one sentence.
- The worked example becomes homework.
- Never cut the toggle-off-then-on sequence on a gameable metric followed by the same toggle on a
  fair one. Without the contrast the demo is a graph; with it, it is the argument.
