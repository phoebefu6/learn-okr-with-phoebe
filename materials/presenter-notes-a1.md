# Presenter notes - Leader session 1: Why we measure, and the trap

Audience: C-level and upper management. No code, ever. This is the session that sets the honesty
of the whole course, so the tone matters more than the content volume.

## Before the session
- Decide in advance whether you will name a real internal metric that got gamed. It lands far
  harder than Cartwheel does, but only say it if you can do it without naming a person.
- Have the cobra effect story ready to tell from memory, not read. It is the one thing everyone
  will still remember next week.
- Know your own answer to "so are you saying we should not measure people?" You will be asked in
  the first ten minutes. The answer is no: measure to steer and grow, and be careful what you
  attach to reward.
- Check whether anyone in the room owns a bonus scheme tied to a single number. If so, expect
  defensiveness at Part 2 and plan to be non-accusatory.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Welcome, what this course is really about
- 3-18: Part 1 - measurement has two faces, steer and grow versus judge
- 18-30: Part 2 - Goodhart, Campbell, the cobra effect
- 30-40: Walk-through 1 - spot the cobra in the sign-ups target
- 40-43: Walk-through 2 - sort three measures into steer, grow, or judge
- 43-45: The honest rule (scores are not pay) + Q&A

## Walk-through run-of-show
- Walk-through 1: do not hand them the answer. Ask "what is the cheapest way to move sign-ups
  without selling anything?" and let the room generate the cobras themselves. A room that invents
  the gaming has understood Goodhart; a room that is told the gaming has only heard it.
- Then show the defence beat: pair the quantity with a quality guardrail (sign-ups that place a
  first order within 30 days). That pairing move recurs in every later session, so plant it here.
- Walk-through 2: the payoff is the conversion-rate line - a great steering signal and a terrible
  individual pay input. That single example carries the whole attribution argument you will make
  properly in a3.

## Watch for
- "We measure everything already, we just need better dashboards." Redirect: the problem is
  usually purpose, not resolution.
- Someone equating "decoupled from pay" with "no accountability." Be explicit: accountability
  stays, it just moves to things the person controls and gets evidenced rather than scored.
- The room treating gaming as a people problem ("we hire honest people"). Repeat that gaming is
  rational response to design. Nobody in the support example cheated.
- Cynicism swinging too far: "so all metrics are useless." Name the two faces again.

## Cuts if long
- Campbell's Law can compress to one line inside the Goodhart explanation.
- Walk-through 2 can go to homework if Walk-through 1 generated a strong discussion.
- Never cut the closing rule that OKR scores are not a pay input. Every later session leans on it,
  and if the room has not heard it here they will argue with a4 and b6 instead.
