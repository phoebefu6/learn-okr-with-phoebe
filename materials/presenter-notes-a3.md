# Presenter notes - Leader session 3: Controllable levers and attribution

Audience: C-level and upper management. Difficulty steps up here. This is the session that
changes how the leadership team assigns goals, so protect the discussion time.

## Before the session
- Find one real shared metric in your organisation that someone is currently graded on alone.
  That single example does more work than the whole framework.
- Re-read a2's metric tree. This session is its consequence, and the room should feel the
  continuity rather than meet a new model.
- Be ready for the political version of the question: "if nobody owns revenue, who is
  accountable?" Prepare a real answer about joint ownership plus per-team levers.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap, where we are
- 3-14: Part 1 - keep splitting until every branch has a name
- 14-28: Part 2 - the attribution problem, and why shared metrics have no fair owner
- 28-38: Walk-through 1 - split the conversion goal into owned levers
- 38-42: Walk-through 2 - grade it, or watch it together?
- 42-45: Q&A

## Walk-through run-of-show
- Walk-through 1: work the conversion goal (2.4% to 3.0%) down to qualified traffic (Marketing),
  add-to-cart (Product), page speed (Engineering). Let the room argue about which team owns page
  speed - that argument IS the attribution problem being felt rather than described.
- Close it the same way every time: the North Star is watched jointly and weekly; each team is
  graded only on its lever at quarter end.
- Walk-through 2 is a fork, not a checklist. For each metric the room decides grade-it or
  watch-it-together, and has to say why. Push back on any "grade it" where the owner cannot move
  the number without waiting on another team.

## Watch for
- The seniority trap: senior leaders will claim they should be graded on the top-line because
  they are accountable for everything. Concede the accountability, refuse the single-number grade.
- Someone splitting the tree so far that leaves become activity counts. Stop at the outcome rung.
- Teams quietly negotiating for the easiest lever. Name it lightly and move on; ambition belongs
  in the target, not in the choice of metric.
- "Watched, not graded" being heard as "ignored." Say explicitly that watched numbers get
  reviewed weekly with everyone in the room.

## Cuts if long
- The Balanced Scorecard reference can drop to a one-line mention.
- Walk-through 2 compresses to two metrics instead of the full set.
- Never cut Walk-through 1. Decomposing a real shared goal into owned levers is the skill this
  session exists to install, and b4 assumes the room can already do it.
