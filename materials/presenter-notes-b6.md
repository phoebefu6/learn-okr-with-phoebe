# Presenter notes - Manager session 6: Fair performance conversations

Audience: middle management and team leads. Difficulty steps to hands-on. This session is about
behaviour, so demonstrate rather than describe wherever possible.

## Before the session
- Have a piece of vague feedback you personally gave once and would now rewrite. Modelling the
  correction yourself sets the tone for a room that has to admit the same thing.
- Know the Adobe, Deloitte and Gallup references as reported findings. Do not overclaim the
  numbers; the argument does not need them inflated.
- Prepare for the CFR question: Conversations, Feedback, Recognition run on their OWN cadence,
  deliberately separate from formal evaluation. The separation is the point, not the acronym.
- Check whether your organisation currently merges the pay conversation into a regular 1:1. If it
  does, decide in advance how frankly you will say so.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap, where we are
- 3-14: Part 1 - frequency beats formality; continuous check-ins; CFRs
- 14-20: Part 2 - the golden rule: keep the three conversations separate
- 20-34: Worked example 1 - turn vague feedback into specific and actionable
- 34-42: Worked example 2 - map a manager's conversations
- 42-45: Q&A

## Worked-example run-of-show
- Worked example 1: start from "be more proactive." Build the replacement in three moves -
  behaviour observed with an example, impact stated, next step offered with support. Then the
  fourth check: is this about the work or the personality? "Proactive" and "abrasive" are labels.
- Take a line from the room and do it live. This is a skill, and skills need one live repetition.
- Worked example 2: three conversations (weekly 1:1, quarterly growth check-in, annual rating and
  pay), each with purpose, cadence, and what the report should feel safe saying. Then trace what
  breaks if the pay decision rides along in the weekly 1:1: the report stops saying "I am stuck."

## Watch for
- "My team knows pay is separate, so it is fine." What matters is what they believe in the moment,
  not what the policy says.
- Feedback rewritten into softer vagueness rather than specifics. Vague-but-kind is still vague.
- Recognition being dismissed as soft. It is the one performance conversation you can safely have
  every week, because it is not evaluation.
- Procedural justice being reduced to tone. It is consistent criteria known in advance, accurate
  information, and a genuine chance to respond.

## Cuts if long
- The CFR breakdown compresses to naming the three and the separate-cadence rule.
- Worked example 2 can run as a spoken walk-through rather than a full table.
- Never cut Worked example 1 done live, or the golden rule. b7 opens assuming the room accepts
  that the conversation itself can be a bias vector.
