# Presenter notes - Leader session 4: Fair-measurement principles

Audience: C-level and upper management. The most uncomfortable session in the track, because the
research says the leaders in the room are themselves the largest source of rating noise. Deliver
it as a design problem, never as an accusation.

## Before the session
- Have the Scullen, Mount and Goff citation and the actual figure ready (roughly 53 to 62 percent
  of variance traced to the rater, depending on rater level). Someone will ask for the source, and
  a vague "research shows" collapses the session.
- Same for the language research: Correll and Simard (HBR 2016) on vague feedback, and the review
  language analysis on personality criticism. Quote as reported, not as universal law.
- If you have permission, pull two anonymised real review lines from your own organisation - one
  all adjectives, one all evidence. Real lines land; invented ones get argued with.
- Decide how you will handle it if a leader in the room recognises their own rating style in the
  lenient or harsh example. Warmth, not gotcha.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap, where we are
- 3-20: Part 1 - the rating measures the rater; the five everyday biases
- 20-30: Part 2 - what fairness actually is; the three justices
- 30-38: Walk-through 1 - spot the rater, not the ratee, in a Cartwheel review
- 38-42: Walk-through 2 - rewrite vague feedback into something usable
- 42-45: Q&A, and the decoupling rule restated

## Walk-through run-of-show
- Walk-through 1 is the centrepiece: same analyst, same work, 4.6 from a lenient manager and 3.2
  from a central-tendency manager. Sit in the 1.4-point gap for a moment before explaining it.
- The critical beat: the fix is NOT averaging two noisy numbers. It is adding process - a second
  reviewer, calibration, evidence tied to specifics, and reporting target plus trend plus context.
  Managers reach for the average instinctively, so pre-empt it.
- Walk-through 2: strip the personality label, name the behaviour and when it happened, state the
  effect, add a next step. Do one live from a line the room supplies.

## Watch for
- Defensiveness dressed as methodology: "our scale is well-defined, so this does not apply."
  Rater variance is about the human, not the scale wording.
- The nihilist response: "so scrap ratings." Flag that removing the visible rating usually moves
  the judgment somewhere less auditable, and that b7 covers this properly.
- Someone treating procedural justice as a communications exercise. It is the process: consistent
  criteria known in advance, accurate information, a real chance to respond.
- Bias being received as an HR compliance topic. Keep it a measurement-quality topic.

## Cuts if long
- The five biases can compress to three named quickly (recency, halo, leniency and severity).
- Walk-through 2 moves to homework.
- Never cut Walk-through 1 or the closing decoupling rule. The two-manager gap is the evidence
  that makes b7 land, and the decoupling rule is the course thesis.
