# Presenter notes - Manager session 1: Foundations

Audience: middle management and team leads. Everyone starts here. Templates and worked examples,
no code, Cartwheel throughout.

## Before the session
- Ask attendees to bring the list of metrics their team is actually measured on today. Worked
  example 2 is far better with real lists than with invented ones.
- Have your own team's metric list ready to show first. Going first makes it safe for them.
- Know the six-point checklist cold - you will refer back to it in every remaining session, and
  b3 scores metrics against exactly these points.
- Be ready for "our metrics are set by leadership, I cannot change them." The answer is that this
  track teaches you what to argue for, and how to keep an unfair metric as context rather than a
  grade.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Welcome, how the track works, the running Cartwheel company
- 3-12: Part 1 - three jobs jammed into one number, and the activity to impact ladder
- 12-18: Part 2 - the six-point fair-metric checklist
- 18-32: Worked example 1 - diagnose and fix tickets closed per day
- 32-42: Worked example 2 - sort your own metrics into the three purposes
- 42-45: Q&A

## Worked-example run-of-show
- Worked example 1: put "grade support agents on tickets closed per day" on the board and ask the
  room to score it against the six points before you show the table. They will find the
  close-and-reopen gaming themselves, which is the point.
- Land the repair explicitly: CSAT paired with first-contact resolution, and tickets closed
  demoted to a workload signal on a dashboard. Fix, do not delete, is the pattern for the track.
- Worked example 2: have them sort their real metrics into steering, development, and evaluation,
  then circle anything doing double duty with pay. The circled items are next week's homework and,
  usually, the whole reason their current system feels unfair.

## Watch for
- People grading themselves on outputs because outputs are what their tooling reports. Name the
  tooling constraint, then aim at the outcome rung anyway.
- The impact rung being chosen as most virtuous. Impact is real but shared and slow; outcomes are
  the fair gradable middle.
- Anyone hearing "decoupled from pay" as a policy they cannot influence. Reframe: you control
  whether your own steering conversations feed the pay conversation.
- Silence at Worked example 2. If nobody will share, use Cartwheel's conversion rate as the
  triple-duty example instead.

## Cuts if long
- The activity-output-outcome-impact ladder can compress to a single spoken sentence per rung.
- Worked example 2 can move to homework.
- Never cut Worked example 1 or the checklist. Every later session assumes the room can score a
  metric on those six points.
