# Presenter notes - Manager session 10: Build your measurement system (capstone)

Audience: middle management and team leads. Hardest session, red difficulty. Almost all of it is
assembly and attack - resist the urge to teach anything new.

## Before the session
- Build the seven layers for your OWN team first and bring it on one page. A capstone where the
  facilitator has not done the exercise is obvious within two minutes.
- Ask attendees to bring their team's OKRs, guardrails and cadence from b2, b4 and b5. Without
  those, this becomes a lecture about a diagram.
- Rehearse the optimiser role. You need to be able to break a system the room presents, live and
  quickly, without it feeling like a gotcha. Attack the design, name the layer, offer the fix.
- Decide what happens after the session. A capstone that ends without a booked quarterly review is
  a nice afternoon.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap - nine sessions of parts, one session of joins
- 3-16: Part 1 - the seven layers, each answering one question; what a missing layer costs
- 16-22: Part 2 - the six gates; red-teaming
- 22-38: Worked example 1 - build the Cartwheel Growth system, all seven layers
- 38-42: Worked example 2 - red-team what you just built
- 42-45: Wrap, homework, and the pointer to the leader track

## Worked-example run-of-show
- Part 1: read the stack as questions rather than components, and use the missing-layer symptoms
  as a diagnostic game - describe a symptom, let the room name the absent layer. Most complaints
  about useless OKRs trace to layer 1 (no goal) or layer 3 (no owned lever), not to wording.
- Worked example 1: build in order and stop at layer 4 to make the point that guardrails are
  written BEFORE the cadence, because they constrain what the target may cost. Then show KR3 as a
  guardrail promoted into the OKR set, because refunds are the thing most likely to be sacrificed
  for conversion. The rule: if a key result can only be hit by damaging something, put that
  something on the page.
- Worked example 2: run all three roles on a real system from the room. Optimiser first (find the
  cheapest route to the number), then employee (where could I be punished for what I do not
  control), then auditor (show me the criteria, the evidence, the decision-maker, the contest
  route). Note out loud that all six Cartwheel fixes required zero new metrics.
- Wrap on gate 1. Wire the score to pay and the other five stop mattering.

## Watch for
- Over-building. Someone will produce twelve guardrails and four objectives. One page or it does
  not exist; mature systems get smaller.
- The auditor pass finding that scoring rules live only in someone's head. That is extremely
  common and is a real finding, not a technicality.
- Red-teaming sliding into naming people who would game it. Attack the design, never the team.
- The room leaving without booking the two recurring meetings. Do it in the room if you can.

## Cuts if long
- The sizing self-study card drops.
- Worked example 2 can run two roles (optimiser and auditor).
- Never cut the optimiser pass or the closing line about gate 1. The optimiser pass is what makes
  the difference between a proofread system and a tested one, and the last beat of the whole track
  is that missing a target has to be safe to say out loud.
