# Presenter notes - Leader session 5: AI in measurement

Audience: C-level and upper management - the people who actually sign off tools. Frame it as a
sign-off decision, not a technology briefing.

## Before the session
- Re-verify the regulatory details the week you deliver. The course map flags this: EU AI Act
  enforcement dates and NYC Local Law 144 specifics move. State them as of a date.
- Have the two cautionary cases straight and attributed: Microsoft Productivity Score (2020, the
  backlash and the subsequent removal of individual-level identification) and Amazon warehouse
  "time off task" as reported by MIT Technology Review. Say "as reported" for the second.
- Ask HR or IT beforehand which tools in your organisation already touch performance data. If you
  can name them in the room, Walk-through 2 becomes a real audit instead of an exercise.
- Prepare your legal disclaimer sentence. You are summarising regulation for awareness, not giving
  legal advice, and you should say so once, clearly, early.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: The question a leader actually has to answer
- 3-18: Part 1 - the line: support augments the manager, surveillance replaces judgment
- 18-28: Part 2 - the three gates (EU AI Act, GDPR Article 22, NYC LL144)
- 28-38: Walk-through 1 - the two cautionary cases, read as a leader
- 38-43: Walk-through 2 - audit your own AI touchpoints against six principles
- 43-45: Q&A

## Walk-through run-of-show
- Walk-through 1: place both cases on the Part 1 line before explaining them. Both sit on the
  surveillance side for the same two reasons: per-person activity data, and automated action on a
  proxy with the human absent or nominal.
- The diagnostic question to leave them with: did the metric measure value delivered, or activity
  observed? Activity is the bottom rung of the ladder wearing better data.
- Walk-through 2: list every touchpoint (drafting reviews, summarising feedback, scoring
  candidates, ranking, flagging activity), then flag anything failing augment-not-replace,
  transparency, or contestability. Those three map to the legal gates, which is why they come first.

## Watch for
- "The vendor says it is compliant." Ask for the bias-audit results and the data behind them. A
  vendor claim is not an audit.
- Someone reading high-risk as banned. High-risk means obligations attached, not prohibition.
- Human-in-the-loop being satisfied by a rubber-stamp approval. Pre-empt the b8 four-minute
  review-round failure: oversight needs the inputs, the time, and the ability to disagree.
- Enthusiasm for activity dashboards because the data is easy to collect. Easy to collect is not
  the same as worth measuring.

## Cuts if long
- The regulation detail compresses to the three one-line gates; leave the specifics to the page.
- Walk-through 1 can run on Microsoft alone.
- Never cut Walk-through 2. Auditing their own touchpoints is the only part that changes what
  they do on Monday.
