# Presenter notes - Manager session 9: When metrics go wrong

Audience: middle management and team leads. Back to medium difficulty after two hard sessions.
This is maintenance work, and the framing is blameless throughout.

## Before the session
- Bring one metric from your own team that broke, and be willing to say what you had rewarded.
  Modelling the blameless post-mortem is the session.
- Have the four modes distinguishable in your own head, because the room will mix them: gaming
  (number up, outcome flat), surrogation (proxy replaces goal, nobody gaming), vanity (nobody
  distorting, number simply useless), single-number verdict (one figure carrying a judgment).
- Know the surrogation citation: Choi, Hecht and Tayler, "Lost in Translation," The Accounting
  Review. Surrogation is the mode people have never heard named, so it needs the source.
- Ask two people privately, before the session, which of their team's numbers they do not trust.
  Anonymised, that is the most powerful five minutes available to you.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap - a broken metric is a design problem, not a people problem
- 3-16: Part 1 - the four failure modes; Goodhart, Campbell, the cobra effect; pairing
- 16-22: Part 2 - the five-step repair kit
- 22-36: Worked example 1 - diagnose three broken Cartwheel metrics
- 36-42: Worked example 2 - the post-mortem and the quarterly metric review
- 42-45: Q&A

## Worked-example run-of-show
- Open with the framing sentence and mean it: the diagnosis never starts with who gamed this, it
  starts with what did we make worth doing. Handling time falling 22 percent while reopens rose 31
  percent is not misconduct; the target asked for exactly what it got.
- Worked example 1: run all three (handling time, the CSAT survey, total registered accounts) and
  make the room name the MODE before the repair. Wrong mode, wrong repair - that is the lesson, so
  do not let anyone jump to the fix.
- The surrogation one is the hard one. The tell is that nobody is cheating: the survey score rose
  because the survey timing changed, while refunds and negative reviews also rose.
- Worked example 2: the five review questions become an agenda item next to the OKR review. Add
  the standing question - which numbers has anyone stopped trusting? Loss of trust precedes the
  data showing a problem.
- Close on the 97 percent on-time-delivery story: accurate for eight months, and wrong, because it
  measured dispatch and the carrier was the problem. Accuracy is not validity.

## Watch for
- Deleting the metric as the reflex fix. The incentive survives the metric; the gaming just moves
  somewhere quieter.
- Blame creeping back in ("our team just does not care about quality"). Return to the incentive.
- Vanity metrics being defended because someone reported them upward last quarter. Name that
  politely as the real obstacle; it usually is.
- Pairs that are not actually in tension. Two numbers that rise together are a dashboard, not a
  control.

## Cuts if long
- The vanity-metric self-study card drops.
- Worked example 1 can run two of the three metrics (keep gaming and surrogation).
- Never cut the mode-before-repair discipline or the quarterly review. The first is the skill, the
  second is the only thing that catches the next broken metric.
