# Presenter notes - Manager session 2: Writing good OKRs

Audience: middle management and team leads. The most immediately practical session in the track,
and the one people will use the same week.

## Before the session
- Collect two draft OKRs from attendees in advance, anonymised. Rewriting a real weak OKR live
  beats rewriting the Cartwheel one.
- Be ready to explain the 0.7 norm with its caveat: a Google convention to adapt, not a rule.
  Someone will otherwise adopt it as a target and then treat 1.0 as failure.
- Have the "done" test phrased as a question you can ask in three words: can you tick it off? If
  yes, it is an output.

## Timing (45 min)
- 0-3: Recap - from measure fairly to write the goal
- 3-20: Part 1 - the Objective inspires, the Key Results measure; the "done" test
- 20-28: Part 2 - committed vs stretch, the 0-1 scale
- 28-40: Worked example 1 - rewrite the weak Cartwheel OKR
- 40-43: Worked example 2 - label OKRs committed or stretch
- 43-45: Q&A

## Worked-example run-of-show
- Worked example 1: show the draft as written ("Objective: do well in Q3. KR: ship the new
  checkout, run 5 marketing campaigns") and let the room diagnose before you do. The "done" test
  does the work: both key results can be ticked off, so both are outputs.
- The rewrite beat is the from-to number: conversion 2.1% to 2.8%, checkout completion 61% to 72%.
  Say out loud that the shift is from proving you were busy to proving something changed.
- Worked example 2: write the expected score next to each key result so nobody is surprised at
  quarter end. Committed targets 1.0, stretch aims for roughly 0.7.

## Watch for
- Key results that are milestones with dates attached. A date does not make an output an outcome.
- Too many key results. Push to two or three. Five is a task list.
- Everything labelled committed (playing safe) or everything stretch (nothing guaranteed). Aim for
  a mix and say so.
- The sandbagging objection. Answer with decoupling: sandbagging is what a pay-linked score buys
  you. Keep returning to it, because this session is where the temptation is strongest.

## Cuts if long
- The scoring mechanics compress to the scale plus the committed-versus-stretch expectation.
- Worked example 2 becomes homework.
- Never cut the "done" test or the from-to rewrite. Those two moves are what people take back to
  their desks.
